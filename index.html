Real ink does not stop at the edge of a letter: it wicks outward along paper fibre, unevenly, faster where the fibre is loose. The effect here is the text's own alpha channel, dilated and pushed through a noise field, drawn underneath the crisp letters so the word stays sharp while its edges bloom.

It costs one offscreen alpha sample and one blurred draw per frame — no fluid solver, no shader. The trick is that the bloom is clipped to a dilated copy of the glyphs, so it can never wander into the margin and read as a smudge.
